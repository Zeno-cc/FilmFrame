## 1. Input assumption and negative-scan inversion

### Confirmed: the main input is a positive scene image, not a camera-negative scan

- The README describes the product as “Open a RAW, pick a film and a paper, and get a physically modelled print” ([README.md L5-L7](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/README.md#L5-L7)).
- On macOS the RAW input is decoded with Apple's tone rendering off into **float32 scene-linear ProPhoto RGB**; a separate display decode is only for the original-image view ([ImageDecoder.swift L11-L20](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/modern_UI/Spektrafilm/Spektrafilm/Import/ImageDecoder.swift#L11-L20)). The app accepts listed RAW formats plus TIFF, PNG, JPEG, HEIC, and EXR ([L109-L113](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/modern_UI/Spektrafilm/Spektrafilm/Import/ImageDecoder.swift#L109-L113)); flat files are treated as already rendered images ([L266-L287](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/modern_UI/Spektrafilm/Spektrafilm/Import/ImageDecoder.swift#L266-L287)). The engine receives linear ProPhoto float32 RGBA and drops alpha ([L317-L349](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/modern_UI/Spektrafilm/Spektrafilm/Import/ImageDecoder.swift#L317-L349)).
- The C ABI likewise accepts caller-owned packed float32 RGB/RGBA pixels, with no negative-density tag or scanner calibration input ([spk_engine.h L66-L73](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/include/spektrafilm/spk_engine.h#L66-L73)).
- The model starts with scene light and synthesizes a latent image, negative dyes, a print, and a scan ([ARCHITECTURE.md L71-L77](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L71-L77)). `run_film` is explicitly `RGB_IN -> CMY_FILM`, producing the developed negative; `run_print` is `CMY_FILM -> RGB_OUT` ([pipeline.hpp L9-L17](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/pipeline.hpp#L9-L17)).
- The engine’s actual session path always derives the negative from the opened source via `run_film`, caches it, then calls `run_print` on it ([engine.cpp L1082-L1120](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/engine.cpp#L1082-L1120), [L1195-L1223](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/engine.cpp#L1195-L1223)). There is no public C ABI entry point that accepts a scan as `CMY_FILM` and skips `run_film` ([spk_engine.h L192-L203, L245-L247](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/include/spektrafilm/spk_engine.h#L192-L203)).

### Confirmed: `scan_film` is an output routing switch, not a camera-negative input mode

- The UI’s “No Print Profile” choice means “scan the developed film instead of printing it”; it shows a negative with its orange mask for a negative stock ([PrintProfileSection.swift L1-L21](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/modern_UI/Spektrafilm/Spektrafilm/Panels/Sections/PrintProfileSection.swift#L1-L21), [L109-L127](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/modern_UI/Spektrafilm/Spektrafilm/Panels/Sections/PrintProfileSection.swift#L109-L127)). Its identifier is `__scan_film__`, not a paper id ([L53-L57](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/modern_UI/Spektrafilm/Spektrafilm/Panels/Sections/PrintProfileSection.swift#L53-L57)).
- In code, enabling `scan_film` skips the paper exposure/development but still performs the spectral **scan of the newly simulated negative**, using that film’s dye densities and viewing illuminant ([pipeline.cpp L322-L338](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/pipeline.cpp#L322-L338), [L1993-L2016](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/pipeline.cpp#L1993-L2016)). Black/white correction is explicitly skipped for a directly scanned negative ([pipeline.cpp L1548-L1557](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/pipeline.cpp#L1548-L1557), [L1683-L1686](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/pipeline.cpp#L1683-L1686)).
- The Digital Intermediate mode also operates on the engine-generated negative: it reads its printing density with the base removed and reverses it on the film’s neutral curve into Cineon-style log ([pipeline.cpp L1638-L1652](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/pipeline.cpp#L1638-L1652); [spk_engine.h L311-L320](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/include/spektrafilm/spk_engine.h#L311-L320)). It is not a decoder for an uploaded negative image.

### Negative-scan answer and unknowns

- **Confirmed:** the normal negative-film-plus-paper pipeline produces a positive print scan, but SpektraLab does **not** provide a camera-negative-scan-to-positive ingestion path in the examined public API/UI. `scan_film` alone does not turn an uploaded orange-mask scan into a positive; feeding such a scan to the normal pipeline would incorrectly treat it as scene RGB and expose it onto fresh film.
- **Not found:** scanner light/source separation, per-channel base estimation, orange-mask removal, or inversion of an imported scan before `run_film`.
- **Unknown:** upstream’s internal `run_print(CMY)` can technically print supplied density values, but that requires calibrated three-channel dye-density data, not arbitrary RGB scanner codes. The examined public session API does not expose this as an import path. Raw camera-negative inversion, scanner characterization, and channel mapping would need separate research; no such support is claimed by this report.

## 2. Ordered exposure, development, print, and scan pipeline

### Confirmed: high-level physical model

The architecture’s order is:

`scene light -> camera + film -> latent image -> development -> negative dye densities -> enlarger + paper -> latent print -> development -> print dye densities -> scanner/viewing -> output RGB`

([ARCHITECTURE.md L71-L77](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L71-L77)). The complete node map is also listed at [ARCHITECTURE.md L118-L145](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L118-L145). Optional nodes can be skipped; slide film bypasses the paper stage.

### Confirmed: spectral arithmetic and spectral-role distinction

- Sampling is 380–780 nm in 5 nm steps: 81 wavelengths. Development curves have 256 log-exposure samples from -3 to 4 ([ARCHITECTURE.md L90-L103](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L90-L103)).
- Hanatos input reconstruction does **not** expand every pixel to 81 channels. RGB becomes XYZ/brightness/chromaticity; a precomputed 192x192x81 spectral table is contracted at setup against film sensitivity to 192x192x3. Per pixel, a bicubic lookup returns three film exposures multiplied by brightness ([ARCHITECTURE.md L93-L103](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L93-L103); [hanatos.hpp L1-L24](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/hanatos.hpp#L1-L24)).
- Film sensitivity is `10^log_sensitivity`, optionally filtered by camera filters/UV/IR and the stock’s Hanatos adaptation window ([hanatos.cpp L159-L200](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/hanatos.cpp#L159-L200), [L212-L234](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/hanatos.cpp#L212-L234)).
- For printing, the film supplies `channel_density` and `base_density`; filtered enlarger light is multiplied by the **paper’s** `10^log_sensitivity` ([printing.cpp L75-L108](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/printing.cpp#L75-L108)). Enlarger C/M/Y dichroic transmittances are applied in CC-density units ([spectral.cpp L70-L101](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/spectral.cpp#L70-L101)).
- Both enlarger and scan use the same three-density-in/three-response-out fused integral. For every wavelength, it computes dye density plus base, transmission `10^-D`, and accumulates illuminant times receiver sensitivity; the wavelength dimension remains in registers ([nodes.metal L185-L211](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/shaders/nodes.metal#L185-L211)). The Vulkan equivalent is [spk_spectral_epilogue.comp L20-L42](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/shaders/vulkan/spk_spectral_epilogue.comp#L20-L42).
- Printing setup also derives exposure normalization from a simulated 0.184 mid-grey through film and paper, optional compensation/pre-flash, and the medium’s black/white references ([printing.cpp L110-L224](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/printing.cpp#L110-L224)). Thus even a “neutral” print setting is film-paper-illuminant dependent.

## 3. Per-stock behavior and what an RGB LUT loses

### Confirmed: measured profile contents

Every profile carries spectral sensitivity, dye-channel density, base density, log-exposure/characteristic curves, layer curves, and a fitted three-layer curve model; film additionally has Hanatos adaptation parameters and halation metadata. See [profile.hpp L35-L65](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/profile.hpp#L35-L65) and the loader’s exact 81-wavelength/curve validation ([profile.cpp L49-L120](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/profile.cpp#L49-L120)). `stage`, not just `support`, distinguishes filming from printing materials; `type` distinguishes negative from positive.

### Confirmed: behavior beyond the profile arrays

- Negative stocks share default DIR coefficient families (with some positive/slide-specific exceptions), but their different measured density curves change the correction. Halation is stock-dependent: still film uses 65 um first sigma, cine 50 um; strong anti-halation strengths are `[0.015,0.005,0]`, weak `[0.08,0.02,0]`, and the no-layer model is different again ([params.cpp L524-L562](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/params.cpp#L524-L562)). Thus Portra and Gold differ in both spectrum/curves and halo strength.
- Default DIR diffusion is 20 um core plus a 200 um tail weighted 0.06 ([params.hpp L110-L122](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/params.hpp#L110-L122)), converted to pixels from film format and source long edge ([pipeline.cpp L1114-L1135](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/pipeline.cpp#L1114-L1135)). This is neighborhood-dependent and density-dependent, not a color transform.
- Grain uses per-channel and per-sublayer development density, exact Poisson-thinned particle counts, separate dye-cloud/microstructure blurs, and physical film pitch ([grain.metal L1-L48](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/shaders/grain.metal#L1-L48); [pipeline.cpp L1328-L1350](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/pipeline.cpp#L1328-L1350), [L1422-L1430](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/pipeline.cpp#L1422-L1430)). It cannot be recovered from final display RGB alone.
- Enlarger filters change the spectrum reaching the paper; exposure compensation/normalization, pre-flash, paper development, and scan/viewing all affect the result ([printing.cpp L75-L224](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/printing.cpp#L75-L224)). Film and paper are independent catalog choices, with declared target pairings tracked separately ([StockCatalog.swift L123-L142](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/modern_UI/Spektrafilm/Spektrafilm/Model/StockCatalog.swift#L123-L142)).
- Output gamut mapping is a perceptual CAM16-UCS operation, including a lightness roll-off and hue-dependent chroma limit, not a fixed matrix ([ARCHITECTURE.md L169-L172](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L169-L172); [gamut.metal L27-L43](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/shaders/gamut.metal#L27-L43)).

### Confirmed: what a fixed/simple RGB LUT approximation loses

A fixed LUT evaluated only on final/display RGB cannot reproduce:

1. The scene-to-film spectral metamerism and stock-specific spectral sensitivity, particularly once input space/illuminant/camera filters change.
2. The intermediate **CMY negative density** and its separate re-printability; the engine deliberately caches this for print edits ([ARCHITECTURE.md L150-L153](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L150-L153)).
3. The two-stage film-then-paper exposure/development and independent paper selection, enlarger filters, exposure normalization, pre-flash, and viewing illuminant.
4. DIR cross-layer inhibition and its 20/200 um spatial diffusion, halation/scatter, lens and scanner blur, and contrast masking.
5. Density-dependent sub-layer stochastic grain and spatial/stochastic print glare.
6. Whole-frame exposure/boost/headroom behavior and subsequent grading/gamut state.
7. Physical format/resolution dependence of grain and diffusion.

**Important distinction:** a 3D LUT can approximate a fixed pointwise subchain. It is not mathematically incapable of encoding all three-channel cross-talk; the loss arises from fixing its input domain, stock pairing, exposure state, output transform, and omitting spatial/global/stochastic stages.

### Confirmed: SpektraLab’s own limited LUT path

- The eight shipped preview LUTs are `33^3 x 3` float32, but their input is **three film-density channels**, not original scene RGB ([print_lut.hpp L1-L21](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/print_lut.hpp#L1-L21)). They cover only print+scan for a particular film-paper bake and omit glare ([Profiles-and-LUTs-CHANGELOG.txt L21-L47](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/modern_UI/Spektrafilm/Spektrafilm/Resources/Licenses/Profiles-and-LUTs-CHANGELOG.txt#L21-L47)).
- They have per-channel density axes and Display P3 output encoding ([print_luts.json L1-L58](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/resources/print_luts.json#L1-L58)). `kodak_portra_endura` is paired to Portra 400; Gold 200 has no dedicated preview LUT.
- A mismatched film is explicitly an approximation with **unmeasured error** because the table is coupled to the negative’s dye spectra ([engine.cpp L1272-L1280](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/engine.cpp#L1272-L1280)).
- Native print-LUT switching is an optimization, not the exact rendering path: the native engine refuses enlarger/scanner LUT approximation settings and evaluates the spectral integral ([pipeline.cpp L143-L151](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/pipeline.cpp#L143-L151)).

## 4. Smallest viable browser-local experiment

### Recommendation: pointwise-first, original TypeScript + Canvas2D reference, optional WebGL2 accelerator

Start with **one positive JPEG/PNG scene image**, one film (**Portra 400**), one declared paper (**Portra Endura**), fixed neutral enlarger settings, fixed exposure/mid-grey calibration, and spatial/stochastic effects disabled. Preserve three stages rather than flattening to an RGB preset:

1. **Film:** decoded scene RGB -> stock-specific Hanatos `tc` lookup -> exposure compensation -> log exposure -> film curves -> cached three-channel negative density. Initially omit DIR correction (or implement it without diffusion as an explicitly different approximation).
2. **Print:** negative density -> exact 81-sample spectral enlarger integral -> paper exposure -> paper curves -> paper density.
3. **Scan:** paper density -> exact 81-sample viewing integral -> XYZ -> chosen output RGB -> simple explicit output transform. Do not label it parity if CAM16, print normalization, glare, unsharp, or other defaults are omitted.

Use TypeScript `Float32Array` for setup and pixel arithmetic, `Math.fround` where float32 behavior matters, and Canvas2D only for input/output display. A Web Worker can keep processing off the UI thread. This is the smallest useful experiment because the exact fused spectral kernels are short and use ordinary scalar arithmetic ([nodes.metal L185-L222](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/shaders/nodes.metal#L185-L222)); it validates stock differences and intermediate densities before GPU complexity.

**Implementation boundary:** independently implement the mathematical model and independently sourced standards data if license separation is required. Do not transliterate the linked C++/Metal/Vulkan implementation into TypeScript/GLSL while calling that license-free. See section 6.

### Option comparison

| Approach | What it can establish | Cost/limitations |
|---|---|---|
| **Pure TS + Canvas2D** | Cleanest local correctness oracle for three density channels, characteristic curves, exact spectral loops, stock/paper differences, and a small preview. No GPU dependency. | JavaScript double arithmetic differs from native float32 kernels unless explicit rounding is used; per-pixel 81-step loops and bicubic lookup can be expensive; Canvas2D `ImageData` is display-oriented, not a linear float processing surface. Benchmark unknown. |
| **WebGL2 fragment passes** | Same pointwise model with parallel per-pixel evaluation; can combine film/print/scan in a small number of passes and cache the negative in a texture. Supports direct spectral loops and LUT sampling. | Requires float render targets (`EXT_color_buffer_float`) and careful linear-float sampling/precision capability checks; `RGBA16F` reduces full-precision exactness. No compute shaders or SSBOs. Blur/IIR/grain and exact Vulkan port are substantial extra work. Browser/device parity unknown. |
| **WASM** | Potential reuse of portable setup mathematics and eventual C ABI; close to native arithmetic; Worker-compatible. | Existing per-pixel engine is GPU-only Metal/Vulkan, not a CPU fallback. `gpu::Gpu` exposes only Metal/Vulkan factories ([gpu.hpp L223-L231](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/gpu/gpu.hpp#L223-L231)); resources use OS file mapping ([blob.cpp L61-L100](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/blob.cpp#L61-L100)). WASM therefore needs a new backend/kernels and resource loading, not just an Emscripten compile. No Emscripten/WebAssembly target was found. Full reuse also triggers GPL obligations (section 6). |
| **WebGPU (not one of the requested three)** | Closest browser analogue to the existing compute kernels, including line-owned IIR recurrence. | Additional platform/API work and no existing WebGPU backend was found; not necessary for the first pointwise experiment. |

**Recommendation:** do not begin with full WASM or a full Vulkan-to-WebGL translation. Prove the pointwise model in TS, add WebGL2 only after a CPU reference exists, then decide whether physical grain/halation justify WebGPU or WASM. This is a staged experiment recommendation, not a claim that WebGL2 can reproduce native parity.

### Browser integration cautions established by the model

- Treat `film density != scanner RGB != display RGB`. Keep the cached negative in a floating-point texture/array; converting it to 8-bit Canvas pixels destroys the print-stage input domain.
- Stock switching changes the Hanatos table, characteristic curves, density spectra and halation preset. Paper switching need not redo film exposure/development; cache the negative as upstream does ([ARCHITECTURE.md L581-L590](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L581-L590)).
- Precompute each film’s contracted `192x192x3` table at build/setup time, not for every frame. If generated from SpektraLab’s baked spectrum/profile assets, clarify their license before redistribution.
- A single 33-cube shipped preview LUT is insufficient for Portra-to-Gold switching or arbitrary scene input: it starts from negative densities and is paired to one film. It can be a separate fast-preview experiment after the film stage exists.
- Browser-local file decode can use `createImageBitmap`/Canvas for JPEG/PNG, but the color space and transfer function must be explicit. Browser camera RAW support is not established by this research. A camera-negative scan needs a different input/calibration feature and must not silently reuse the positive-scene path.

## 5. Performance and resource constraints

### Confirmed native measurements (not browser promises)

- At 45 MP on an M3 Max, warm: live 1600 first 0.13 s/reprint 0.01 s; preview 3400 first 0.25 s/reprint 0.04 s; full 7800x5800 first 0.87 s/reprint 0.17 s. Print-LUT flips are 2.0/9.5/47 ms respectively ([ARCHITECTURE.md L615-L625](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L615-L625)). Another 45 MP sequence with grain/glare off reports 6.2 ms at 1600, 13.7 ms at 2560, and 121.7 ms at 8192 for live reprint ([ARCHITECTURE.md L323-L334](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L323-L334)). These are different configurations and should not be conflated.
- Opening a 24 MP RAW is about 2.0 s, 1.6 s in Core Image; browser decode costs will differ ([ARCHITECTURE.md L625-L626](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L625-L626)).
- The original 45 MP reference profile spent much of its 12.9 s in halation and DIR diffusion, the two large spatial nodes ([ARCHITECTURE.md L239-L247](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L239-L247)). This supports leaving spatial effects out of the first experiment, but gives no browser timing.
- A 102 MP frame peaked at 13.7 GB in native version 1.0.3; native uses full-frame intermediate reuse and only one full-resolution render at a time ([ARCHITECTURE.md L176-L188](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L176-L188)). The native hard input limit is 151,301,008 pixels, but this is not a safe browser target ([engine.cpp L92-L103](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/engine.cpp#L92-L103)).
- Setup cache: Hanatos table contraction is 192x192x81, plus ray-polygon remapping; CAM16 is 64 lightnesses x 720 hues x 18 inverse evaluations, about 830,000 inversions ([setup_cache.hpp L10-L22](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/setup_cache.hpp#L10-L22)). Upstream reports 160 ms optimized/250 ms debug for these repeated setup products in its environment; not a JS/WASM benchmark.
- Blur uses exact-seeming native choices of separable FIR below sigma 3 and Young–van Vliet IIR at sigma >=3, decided per channel ([blur.hpp L1-L11](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/blur.hpp#L1-L11); [numeric.hpp L80-L95](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/core/numeric.hpp#L80-L95)). IIR is line-carried and prevents simple independent tiles. Spatial mixtures hold multiple full-frame scratch buffers ([blur.cpp L239-L268](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/engine/src/pipeline/blur.cpp#L239-L268)).
- Native parity reports 27 one-megapixel render configurations at maximum error 2.3e-5 and a 3e-5 absolute render bar ([ARCHITECTURE.md L592-L609](https://github.com/JamesQiu2005/SpektraLab/blob/02d82b56cf65f0f8e802a708612edc4e9a4790af/ARCHITECTURE.md#L592-L609)). No browser parity exists.

### Unknowns

- No JS, WebGL2, WebGPU, WASM, mobile-browser, or browser memory benchmark was found or run.
- No validated simplified-model error against the native full pipeline exists for the proposed omissions.
- The smallest acceptable preview size and acceptable approximation are product decisions, not established by upstream evidence.
- Browser `RGBA16F` rendering/filtering behavior, cross-device floating-point parity, maximum texture sizes, and worker/WASM memory ceilings need device testing.

## Caveats / Not Found

- This was static source research only. No code, browser prototype, native application, parity harness, or benchmark was executed.
- No camera-negative-scan inversion/import feature was found; ordinary scene-positive input is the confirmed contract. A scan-conversion feature needs separate scanner/base/densitometry research.
- No Emscripten/WASM or WebGL/WebGPU backend was found. Existing Vulkan kernels do not make the engine directly browser-compatible.
- No browser performance, resource ceiling, cross-device numerical parity, or simplified-model accuracy was established. Native M3 Max timings and memory are context, not predictions.
- Individual license/provenance of the Hanatos irradiance table and every baked color-science constant was not established. The original profile CC BY-SA notice and SpektraLab GPL are clear, but that does not settle every constant’s reuse terms.
- The proposed TS-first pipeline deliberately omits spatial/stochastic behavior and possibly exact print normalization/CAM16. It must be described as a **pointwise spectral film/print experiment**, not a full SpektraLab port or parity implementation.
- Legal observations are source-grounded risk flags, not legal advice.
